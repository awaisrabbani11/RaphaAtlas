Which tool answers which question

Each calculator here solves one specific problem and states the formula it uses. None of them is a diagnostic instrument, and none replaces a clinician who can see your history. Used for what they are — structured estimates that save you arithmetic — they are genuinely useful.

CalculatorAnswersMethod
Macro Calculator How many calories, and how much protein, carbohydrate and fat, should I eat per day? Mifflin-St Jeor by default, with Harris-Benedict and Katch-McArdle available. Resting energy expenditure is multiplied by an activity factor to give total daily expenditure, then adjusted for your goal.
Body Type Calculator Which somatotype does my frame most resemble — ectomorph, mesomorph, or endomorph? Classification from bone structure and body measurements, following the somatotype tradition originated by Sheldon and later put on an anthropometric footing by Heath and Carter.
BAC Calculator Roughly what blood alcohol concentration would these drinks produce over this time? The Widmark formula, which divides alcohol consumed by body weight and a sex-based distribution constant, then subtracts elimination over elapsed time.
Conception Calculator When did conception most likely occur, and when is the due date? Naegele's rule applied to your last menstrual period, adjusted for cycle length, or worked backwards from a known due date.

What a calculator can and cannot tell you

Every formula on this page was derived from a population, and populations have variance. Mifflin-St Jeor predicts resting metabolic rate within about ten percent for most people — which means a meaningful minority sit outside that band, and a small number sit well outside it. That is not a flaw in the equation; it is what a population regression is.

The practical consequence is that a calculator output is a starting hypothesis, not a measurement. Take the number, apply it consistently for two to three weeks, observe what actually happens, and adjust from the observation. Someone whose weight holds steady on a calculated 300-calorie deficit has learned something more accurate about their own metabolism than any equation could have told them.

The BAC calculator in particular

The Widmark formula ignores food in the stomach, individual differences in the enzymes that metabolise alcohol, medication, liver function, and the fact that drink strengths and home pours vary enormously. Real-world error is large in both directions. Never use this or any estimator to decide whether you are safe to drive. If you have been drinking, do not drive.

Two more limits worth stating plainly. Somatotype describes a body's current shape; it does not set a ceiling on what training and nutrition can change, and the older literature claiming it predicts athletic destiny does not hold up. And conception dating from a last menstrual period assumes ovulation around day 14, which is an average rather than a rule — ultrasound dating in the first trimester is considerably more accurate, which is exactly why clinics use it.

Common questions

Are these calculators free, and do I need an account?

All of them are free and none requires an account or sign-in. They run in your browser, so the figures you enter are used to produce your result and are not submitted anywhere.

Why do different websites give me different calorie numbers?

Because they use different equations and different activity multipliers. Mifflin-St Jeor, Harris-Benedict, and Katch-McArdle each estimate resting metabolic rate differently, and Katch-McArdle additionally needs a body fat percentage. Activity multipliers vary even more between sites, since "moderately active" has no standard definition. Pick one method, use it consistently, and judge it by results over weeks.

Which calorie formula should I choose?

Mifflin-St Jeor is the sensible default and generally the most accurate for the general population. Katch-McArdle can be better if you know your body fat percentage from a reliable measurement, because it works from lean mass — but a guessed body fat figure makes it worse, not better. Harris-Benedict is the oldest of the three and tends to overestimate.

Can I use the BAC calculator to check if I can drive?

No. It is an educational estimate with substantial error in both directions, and it cannot account for your metabolism, medication, food intake, or the actual strength of what you drank. No calculator is a defence and none is a substitute for not driving after drinking.

How accurate is a due date from the conception calculator?

It is a reasonable estimate when cycles are regular, but only a small percentage of births occur on the calculated date — most fall within a couple of weeks either side. Irregular cycles or uncertainty about the last period widen the range considerably. First-trimester ultrasound gives a more accurate date and is what maternity care relies on.
